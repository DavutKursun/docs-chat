# Aria X200 Air Purifier — User Manual

Model X200 · Document revision 2.1

## 1. Safety first

Read this manual before using the device. The Aria X200 is intended for indoor household use only. Do not use it in bathrooms or other humid rooms, and keep it at least 30 cm away from walls and curtains so that air can flow freely. Unplug the device before cleaning or replacing the filter. Do not insert objects into the air outlet. The device is not a toy; keep children under 8 years away from it.

### 1.1 Electrical safety

Connect the device only to a grounded wall socket that matches the rated voltage shown on the label under the device. Do not use the device if the power cord, the plug or the housing is damaged; contact support for a replacement. Do not run the cord under carpets or across walkways, and do not pull on the cord to unplug the device. Avoid extension cords; if one is unavoidable, use a single extension cord rated for at least 10 A and never connect several in a row. Unplug the device during thunderstorms and when it will not be used for more than a week. Repairs may only be carried out by the manufacturer's service centre; opening the housing voids the warranty.

### 1.2 Placement and operating safety

Keep the air inlet on the back and the air outlet on top free at all times; do not place objects, clothes or towels on the device. Do not operate the device next to open flames, candles, gas appliances or heaters, and do not use it to remove smoke from a fire. Do not use the device in rooms where aerosols, paint or flammable products are being used, and keep it away from oily kitchen fumes, which clog the filter quickly. If liquid gets into the device, unplug it immediately and contact support before using it again. Persons with reduced physical, sensory or mental abilities may use the device only under supervision or after instruction in its safe use.

### 1.3 Intended use

The Aria X200 removes airborne particles and odours from the air of a single room. It is not a medical device, it does not replace ventilation, and it does not remove carbon monoxide or other gases that are dangerous in high concentrations. Any use other than the one described in this manual is considered misuse.

### 1.4 Symbols used in this manual and on the device

A triangle with an exclamation mark marks a safety warning that must be followed to avoid injury or damage. A circled letter i marks a note that helps you get the best out of the device. On the rating label, the double square symbol indicates a device with reinforced insulation, the CE mark indicates conformity with European requirements, and the crossed-out wheeled bin indicates separate collection at the end of life. The arrow on the filter frame shows the direction of airflow and must point up when the filter is installed.

## 2. What is in the box

The box contains the Aria X200 unit, one pre-installed 3-stage filter (pre-filter, HEPA H13 filter and activated carbon layer), a power cord, a remote control with two AAA batteries, and this manual. If any item is missing, contact the retailer within 14 days of purchase.

### 2.1 Unpacking and checking the device

Lift the device out of the box by the recessed handles on both sides, never by the rear cover. Remove all tape and foam. Check the housing for transport damage and note the serial number from the label under the device in the space provided on the last page of this manual; you will need it for warranty claims. Keep the original packaging for at least the warranty period in case the device has to be sent to the service centre.

### 2.2 Accessories and spare parts

The following accessories are available from the online shop: replacement 3-stage filters (see section 7), the RC-20 remote control, and the CB-10 castor base, which lets you roll the device between rooms. Use only original Aria filters; filters from other manufacturers may not seal properly, which lets unfiltered air pass around the filter and can damage the fan.

### 2.3 Overview of the device

The air inlet with the filter compartment is on the back of the device, behind the rear cover. Clean air leaves through the outlet grille on top, which also carries the control panel and the display. The light ring runs around the display. The air quality sensor sits behind a small opening on the right-hand side, protected by a removable dust cap that must be taken off before the first use. The rating label with the serial number, the model number and the electrical data is on the underside of the housing, and the recessed handles for carrying are on both sides. The cord leaves the device at the lower back.

### 2.4 Registering your device

You may register your device on the manufacturer's website within 30 days of purchase by entering the serial number and the date of purchase. Registration is optional and does not change the warranty terms, but it speeds up warranty claims because support can then look up your purchase without a copy of the receipt. Registered owners also receive a reminder when the recommended filter replacement date approaches.

## 3. Technical specifications

The Aria X200 covers rooms up to 45 square metres. Its clean air delivery rate (CADR) is 320 cubic metres per hour. Power consumption is 6 W in sleep mode and 42 W at the highest fan speed. Noise level ranges from 22 dB in sleep mode to 50 dB at the highest speed. The device weighs 5.8 kg and measures 24 cm by 24 cm by 54 cm. Rated voltage is 220–240 V, 50/60 Hz.

### 3.1 Airflow and power by fan speed

The fan has five manual speeds. Airflow is approximately 70 cubic metres per hour at speed 1, 120 at speed 2, 190 at speed 3, 260 at speed 4 and 320 at speed 5. Power consumption rises accordingly from about 9 W at speed 1 to 13 W, 19 W and 28 W at speeds 2 to 4; the values for sleep mode and for the highest speed are given above. At speed 3 the device exchanges the air of a 20 square metre room with a standard ceiling height roughly twice per hour, which is enough for everyday use; use the higher speeds while cooking or when the indicator is red.

### 3.2 Operating conditions

The Aria X200 is designed for indoor use at room temperatures between 5 °C and 40 °C. Below the lower limit the fan motor may not start, and above the upper limit the device switches off to protect its electronics (see the error codes in section 9). Do not expose the device to direct sunlight for long periods, as heat builds up inside the housing and the air quality sensor may give high readings. The device is designed for altitudes up to 2,000 metres.

### 3.3 Filter specifications

The 3-stage filter combines a pre-filter mesh that catches hair, dust and lint and can be vacuumed or wiped, a HEPA H13 layer that retains at least 99.95 percent of particles of 0.3 micrometres, and a layer of about 350 grams of activated carbon that adsorbs odours and volatile organic compounds. The filter cartridge measures 22 cm by 22 cm by 6 cm and weighs about 0.9 kg. The HEPA and carbon layers are replaced together as one cartridge.

### 3.5 Sensor specifications

The air quality sensor is a laser scattering sensor that measures particles between 0.3 and 10 micrometres and reports the PM2.5 fraction. Its measuring range is 0 to 500 micrograms per cubic metre with a resolution of 1 microgram per cubic metre; the accuracy is plus or minus 10 micrograms per cubic metre below 100 and plus or minus 10 percent above that value. The reading on the display is updated every three seconds and smoothed over the last 30 seconds so that single dust puffs do not make the fan speed jump. The sensor does not need calibration and is designed for a service life of at least eight years; a sensor that drifts or fails is reported with an error code.

### 3.4 Dimensions and clearances

With the recommended clearance of 30 cm on all sides, the device needs a floor area of about 85 cm by 85 cm. The air outlet on top must have at least 50 cm of free space above it. The power socket should be within reach of the cord without strain; do not place the device so that the cord is pulled tight or hangs in the air.

## 4. Getting started

Place the device on a flat, stable surface. Remove the plastic bag from the filter before first use: open the rear cover, pull out the filter, remove the bag, and put the filter back with the arrow pointing up. Close the cover until it clicks. Connect the power cord and press the power button. The device starts in Auto mode.

### 4.1 Choosing the right place

For the best result, place the device in the room where you spend the most time, such as the bedroom or the living room, and keep doors and windows closed while it runs. Keep the recommended distance from walls and furniture so that the clean air can spread. Do not place the device on a shelf or a cupboard; it should stand on the floor or on a low, stable table. In large open-plan rooms the device cleans the air around it first; move it closer to the main source of pollution, such as the kitchen door, if the indicator stays yellow or red.

### 4.2 First run

When you switch the device on for the first time, a new-filter smell may be noticeable for the first 48 hours; this is normal and disappears with use. During the first minutes the display shows two dashes while the sensor warms up. Run the device in Turbo mode after unpacking to clear the room quickly, then leave it in Auto mode. Check after a day that the indicator is green most of the time; if it is not, see section 6.

### 4.3 Using the device in different rooms

In the bedroom, run the device in Auto mode during the day and in Sleep mode at night; place it at least one metre from the bed. In the living room, Auto mode is sufficient; switch to Turbo mode after guests have left or after lighting candles. In a home office, speed 2 is a good compromise between airflow and quiet. In a child's room, activate the child lock described in section 5 and keep the device out of reach of small children. The device should not be used in a kitchen without a cooker hood, because grease shortens the life of the filter considerably.

### 4.4 The first week

During the first days the device works harder than usual because it is removing the dust that has settled in the room over time; the indicator may show yellow for longer periods and the fan may run at higher speeds in Auto mode. This settles after a few days. The filter indicator will not come on in the first weeks under normal conditions. If the indicator stays red for most of the day during the first week, look for a strong source of particles in the room, such as a fireplace, an open window on a busy street or a cooker without a hood.

## 5. Operating modes

Auto mode adjusts the fan speed to the air quality measured by the built-in sensor. Sleep mode runs at the lowest speed with the display dimmed and is recommended for bedrooms. Turbo mode runs at the highest speed for 30 minutes and then returns to Auto mode. The fan speed can also be set manually from 1 to 5. The timer can switch the device off after 1, 2, 4 or 8 hours.

### 5.1 Control panel and buttons

The control panel on top of the device has four touch buttons. The power button switches the device on and off. The mode button cycles through Auto, Sleep and Turbo. The fan button sets the manual speed from 1 to 5; pressing it leaves Auto mode. The timer button sets the switch-off timer; the selected time appears on the display and counts down. Each button lights up when its function is active. A short beep confirms each press; the beep can be switched off by holding the mode button for 3 seconds and switched on again in the same way.

### 5.2 Child lock

To prevent children from changing the settings, hold the timer button for 3 seconds until the lock icon appears on the display. While the lock is active, the buttons on the device do not respond, but the remote control still works. Hold the timer button for 3 seconds again to unlock. The lock is cleared when the device is unplugged.

### 5.3 Auto mode in detail

In Auto mode the device checks the sensor reading every few seconds and chooses the fan speed from it: speed 1 while the air quality is good, speed 3 while it is moderate, and speed 5 while it is poor. To avoid constant switching, the speed is only lowered after the reading has stayed below a threshold for two minutes. Auto mode is the recommended setting for everyday use because it keeps the air clean with the lowest possible noise and energy consumption.

### 5.4 Timer

Press the timer button repeatedly to cycle through 1, 2, 4 and 8 hours; a fifth press cancels the timer. The remaining time is shown on the display in whole hours and counts down. When the timer ends, the device switches off completely and remembers the mode it was in. The timer can be combined with any mode; a common combination is Sleep mode with the 8-hour timer at bedtime.

### 5.5 Display brightness

Press the mode button briefly twice within one second to cycle the display brightness through high, low and off. In Sleep mode the display dims automatically and switches off after 30 seconds; it lights up again briefly when a button is pressed or the remote control is used. The light ring described in section 6 follows the display setting, so it is also off when the display is off.

### 5.6 Remote control

The remote control uses infrared light and must be pointed at the front of the device; its range is about 5 metres with a clear line of sight. It mirrors the buttons on the device and adds a dedicated Sleep button and a light button for the display brightness. If the device reacts slowly or only from close by, replace the two AAA batteries. Remove the batteries if the remote control will not be used for several months, and keep the remote control away from heat and moisture.

### 5.7 Sleep mode in detail

Sleep mode is designed for bedrooms. The fan runs at its lowest speed, which is barely audible from a distance of two metres, and the device makes no beeps while Sleep mode is active. The display and the light ring dim and then switch off, as described above, so that no light disturbs your sleep. The sensor keeps measuring, but the fan speed is not raised automatically; if the air quality becomes poor during the night, the device stays quiet and shows the reading when you press a button. Many users combine Sleep mode with the 8-hour timer so that the device switches off by itself in the morning.

### 5.8 Which mode for which situation

Use Auto mode as the default whenever you are at home. Use Turbo mode after cooking, after cleaning, after lighting candles or when guests have left, to clear the room quickly before the device returns to Auto mode on its own. Use Sleep mode at night or during video calls. Use a fixed manual speed when you want a constant noise level, for example speed 2 for background noise while working, or speed 4 or 5 on days with heavy outdoor pollution. Use the timer when you want the device to switch off after you fall asleep or after you leave the room.

## 6. Air quality indicator

The light ring on the front shows the air quality: green means good (PM2.5 below 35 µg/m³), yellow means moderate (35 to 75 µg/m³), and red means poor (above 75 µg/m³). The numeric PM2.5 value is shown on the display. The sensor needs about 3 minutes after power-on to give a stable reading.

### 6.1 What PM2.5 means

PM2.5 stands for particulate matter with a diameter of 2.5 micrometres or less, about one thirtieth of the width of a human hair. These particles come from traffic, heating, cooking, candles, tobacco smoke and pollen, and they stay in the air for hours because they are too light to settle. The value on the display is given in micrograms per cubic metre and is measured by a laser sensor that draws a small sample of room air through the sensor opening every few seconds. The thresholds of the light ring follow the 24-hour guideline values used by common air quality indices.

### 6.2 Typical readings and what to do

A reading below 10 is typical for a well-ventilated room without sources of pollution. Values rise quickly while cooking, especially when frying, and when candles or a fireplace are lit; they fall again within an hour in Auto mode. If the indicator stays red for more than an hour while no source is visible, check that the sensor opening is clean (see section 8) and that the filter does not need replacing. Outdoor air may be the source: on days with high outdoor pollution, keep the windows closed and let the device run continuously.

### 6.3 Seasonal advice

In spring, pollen enters through open windows and is captured by the HEPA layer; people with hay fever should ventilate briefly in the early morning and keep the device running afterwards. In winter, heating and candles raise indoor particle levels while windows stay closed, so the filter may fill faster than in summer. During episodes of wildfire smoke or smog, run the device at speed 4 or 5 with the windows closed and check the pre-filter weekly.

### 6.4 Reading the highest value of the day

The device stores the highest PM2.5 reading of the last 24 hours. Press the mode and fan buttons together to show it on the display for five seconds, followed by the number of hours since it was recorded. This helps you find out what causes high readings when you are not in the room, for example cooking in the evening or traffic in the morning. The stored value is cleared when the device is unplugged.

### 6.5 Why indoor readings can be higher than outdoor readings

Indoor air is often more polluted than the air outside because particles are produced inside, in a small volume, with little dilution: frying, toasting, candles, a fireplace, vacuuming, printing and tobacco smoke all raise the reading quickly, and in a closed room the particles have nowhere to go. Outdoor readings published by monitoring stations are averages over an hour or a day at a different place. Do not be alarmed by short peaks; what matters for health is the average exposure over hours and days, which the device keeps low in Auto mode.

## 7. Filter care

The pre-filter should be vacuumed or wiped every two weeks. The 3-stage filter must be replaced every 6 months under normal use, or sooner if the filter indicator turns on. Replacement filters are sold under the part number AF-200. To reset the filter indicator after replacing the filter, hold the fan button for 5 seconds until the indicator light turns off. Do not wash the HEPA filter; water destroys the filter material.

### 7.1 How the filter indicator works

The filter indicator counts the running hours of the fan, weighted by the fan speed, and estimates the remaining filter life from them. Under normal use, which the manufacturer defines as 12 hours per day in Auto mode, the indicator comes on after about six months; in a dusty environment or with permanent Turbo use it comes on earlier. The remaining filter life in percent is shown on the display for a few seconds when you press the fan and timer buttons together. The indicator cannot measure how dirty the filter actually is, so replace the filter earlier if the airflow has noticeably dropped or odours are no longer removed.

### 7.2 Replacing the filter step by step

Switch the device off and unplug it. Open the rear cover by pressing the two latches at the top. Pull the old filter out by its fabric tab and put it in a bag straight away to avoid spreading dust. Remove the new filter from its plastic bag, insert it with the arrow pointing up and the fabric tab facing you, and close the cover until it clicks. Plug the device in, switch it on and reset the filter indicator as described at the start of this section. Dispose of the old filter as described in the disposal section at the end of this manual.

### 7.3 Storing spare filters

Spare filters keep for up to 2 years when stored sealed in their original packaging in a dry place at room temperature. Do not open the packaging until you install the filter, because the activated carbon starts to adsorb odours as soon as it is exposed to air. Write the installation date on the label on the side of the filter when you install it, so that you can check the indicator against the calendar.

### 7.4 Filter life in demanding environments

Homes with pets, smokers or an open fireplace, and rooms next to a busy road, load the filter faster. In such environments, expect to replace the filter after three to four months and vacuum the pre-filter weekly instead of every two weeks. After renovation work, dust can block the filter within days; run the device only after the coarse dust has been removed.

### 7.5 How the three filter stages work

The pre-filter mesh is the first stage. It catches hair, lint and coarse dust, which protects the HEPA layer from clogging and is why it should be cleaned regularly. The HEPA layer is a dense mat of very fine fibres; particles are caught in it by sticking to the fibres as the air passes through, and the layer becomes slightly more efficient as it loads up, until the resistance to airflow becomes too high. The activated carbon layer works differently: its enormous internal surface traps odour molecules and volatile organic compounds by adsorption. Carbon saturates with time regardless of how dirty the air looks, which is why the filter must be replaced on schedule even when it does not appear dirty.

## 8. Cleaning

Wipe the outside of the device with a soft, dry cloth. Clean the air quality sensor opening on the side of the device with a cotton swab every month. Do not use alcohol, solvents or abrasive cleaners.

### 8.1 Cleaning schedule

Clean the outlet grille on top of the device every three months with a soft brush or the brush attachment of a vacuum cleaner. Wipe the remote control with a dry cloth; do not spray cleaning products on it. The pre-filter is covered in section 7 and the sensor opening at the start of this section. Never spray water or cleaning fluid into the air inlet or outlet, and let the device dry completely if the housing has been wiped with a damp cloth.

### 8.2 Removing stubborn dirt

If dry wiping is not enough, dampen the cloth with water and a drop of mild dish soap, wring it out well and wipe the housing, then dry it with a second cloth. Never let water run into the outlet grille, the sensor opening or the control panel. The outlet grille is not removable; use a soft brush between the slats. Stickers and tape leave residue on the housing and must not be used. Do not use disinfectant sprays on the device; if disinfection is required, wipe the housing with a cloth dampened with the disinfectant and let it dry completely before switching the device on.

## 9. Troubleshooting and error codes

If the device does not turn on, check that the rear cover is closed properly; a safety switch prevents operation while the cover is open. If airflow is weak, check the filter and remove the plastic bag if it is still in place.

Error code E1 means the fan motor is blocked: unplug the device, check for obstructions and restart. Error code E2 means the air quality sensor is dirty or faulty: clean the sensor opening; if the error persists, contact support. Error code E3 means the device has overheated: unplug it and let it cool for 30 minutes before restarting. Error code E5 means the filter is not detected: open the rear cover and reseat the filter.

### 9.1 Further error codes

Error code E4 means that the control board cannot communicate with the display: unplug the device, wait one minute and restart it; if the code returns, contact support. Error code E6 means that the fan speed sensor reports no rotation although the motor is running: unplug the device and contact support, as the fan unit needs to be checked. Error code E7 means that the room temperature is outside the operating range of the device: move it to a warmer or cooler place and restart it once the temperature is back within the range given in section 3. If the filter indicator flashes instead of staying on, the filter was replaced but the indicator was not reset.

### 9.2 Common questions about behaviour

A slight smell after unpacking is normal and disappears within two days. A rattling noise usually means the filter is not seated correctly or the packaging is still in place; open the rear cover and check. Two dashes on the display mean that the sensor is warming up. The device returning to Auto mode on its own is expected after Turbo mode has finished. If the fan speed changes frequently in Auto mode, the sensor is reacting to real changes in air quality, for example from cooking in a neighbouring room. If the display is dark but the fan is running, the display brightness is set to off (see section 5).

### 9.3 Factory reset

To restore all settings to the factory defaults, hold the power and timer buttons for 10 seconds until the display shows three dashes and the device restarts. The factory reset clears the timer, the display brightness, the child lock and the beep setting. It does not reset the filter indicator; use the procedure in section 7 for that.

### 9.4 When to contact support

Contact support if an error code returns after the steps above, if the device makes a grinding noise, if the housing becomes unusually hot, or if the airflow stays weak with a new filter. Do not continue to use a device that smells of burning or shows visible damage.

### 9.6 Error history

The device stores the last three error codes together with the number of running hours at which they occurred. To show them, press the mode and timer buttons together three times within two seconds; the display then shows the codes one after another, newest first, each followed by the hour count. This information helps support to diagnose intermittent faults, so have it ready when you call. The error history is kept through power cuts and is cleared only by a factory reset.

### 9.5 Problems without an error code

The device does not react to the buttons: the child lock may be active (a lock icon is shown) or the buttons may be wet; dry the control panel with a cloth. The device switches off by itself: the timer may be running, or Turbo mode has ended and the device has returned to Auto mode at a low speed that you do not hear. The indicator is always green although the room smells: the sensor measures particles, not gases, so odours are not shown; Turbo mode still removes them through the carbon layer. The indicator is red immediately after switching on: the sensor is still warming up, wait for a stable reading. The remote control does not work: point it directly at the front of the device, remove obstacles, and replace the batteries. There is a draught near the device: this is the outlet air; move the device or turn it so that the outlet does not point at your seat.

## 10. Energy use

Running costs depend mainly on the fan speed. In a typical home where the device runs in Auto mode for about eight hours a day, it consumes roughly 70 kilowatt-hours per year. Running it continuously at the highest speed would consume about 370 kilowatt-hours per year. The device draws less than 1 W in standby when it is switched off with the power button but still plugged in. To save energy, use Auto mode, let the timer switch the device off at night, and keep the filter clean so that the fan does not have to work harder than necessary.

### 10.1 Noise

The lowest speed is barely audible and suitable for sleeping; speed 2 is comparable to a quiet library, speed 3 to a normal conversation at some distance, and speed 5 to a running kitchen extractor. Noise increases if the filter is dirty, because the fan has to work harder, and if the device stands on a hard, resonant surface. Placing the device on a rug or a felt mat reduces vibration noise. A rattling or grinding noise is not normal; see the troubleshooting section.

## 11. Transport and storage

Before transporting the device, unplug it, remove the filter and pack both separately, ideally in the original packaging. Carry the device upright by the recessed handles. For storage, clean the device, remove the batteries from the remote control and store everything in a dry place at temperatures between minus 10 °C and 50 °C. After storage, let the device reach room temperature before switching it on.

## 12. Maintenance summary

Every two weeks: vacuum or wipe the pre-filter. Every month: clean the sensor opening with a cotton swab. Every three months: clean the outlet grille. Every six months, or when the indicator comes on: replace the 3-stage filter and reset the indicator. Once a year: check the power cord and plug for damage and replace the batteries of the remote control. Keep a note of the dates in the table on the last page of this manual.

### 12.1 Indoor air quality tips

An air purifier is most effective together with good habits. Ventilate briefly but thoroughly twice a day, ideally when outdoor pollution is low, and keep the windows closed otherwise so that the device can clean the room air. Use the cooker hood when cooking and keep the kitchen door closed. Avoid burning candles and incense in rooms where the device runs, or switch to Turbo mode afterwards. Choose low-emission paints, cleaning products and furniture, and let new furniture air out before placing it in a bedroom. Vacuum with a cleaner that has a fine-dust filter, because ordinary vacuum cleaners blow fine particles back into the room. Do not smoke indoors.

### 12.2 Children and pets

Place the device where small children cannot pull on the cord or reach the air outlet, activate the child lock described in section 5, and explain to older children that nothing may be put into the outlet grille. Pets are not harmed by the device, but their hair and dander load the pre-filter quickly; in homes with pets, clean the pre-filter weekly and expect a shorter filter life, as described in section 7. Some pets are startled by the fan starting up in Auto mode; a fixed low speed avoids sudden changes.

### 12.3 Accessibility

The display uses large white digits on a dark background and the light ring can be seen from across the room. Every button press is confirmed by a beep unless the beep has been switched off, and the buttons on the remote control have raised symbols so that they can be told apart by touch. The child lock and the display brightness can both be changed from the remote control, so the device can be operated without bending down to the control panel.

## 13. Frequently asked questions

Can I run the device all day and night? Yes. The Aria X200 is designed for continuous operation. Use Sleep mode at night and leave Auto mode on during the day.

Does the device produce ozone? No. The Aria X200 cleans the air mechanically with filters and does not generate ozone.

Can I use the device in the kitchen? Yes, but keep it away from the hob, because oily fumes clog the filter quickly, and expect the filter to need replacing sooner.

Why does the indicator turn red when I vacuum? Vacuuming stirs up dust and fine particles; the reading falls again after a few minutes.

Can I use the device with the windows open? The device still works, but it has to compete with incoming air, so the effect is limited. Close the windows for the best result.

Is the device suitable for people with allergies? The HEPA layer retains pollen, dust mite allergens and pet dander. Run the device in the bedroom for an hour before going to bed and keep it in Sleep mode overnight.

Can I move the device between rooms? Yes. Unplug it first and carry it by the recessed handles on the sides; do not carry it by the rear cover. The optional castor base makes moving easier.

Can the filter be washed? No, the HEPA and carbon layers must not get wet; only the pre-filter mesh can be vacuumed or wiped. See section 7.

How do I know a filter is genuine? Original filters carry the Aria hologram label and a serial number on the side of the cartridge.

What happens during a power cut? The device remembers its last mode and restarts in that mode when power returns; the timer is cleared.

Why is the reading on the display different from the outdoor value reported by the weather service? Outdoor stations measure at a different place and time; indoor values depend on what happens in the room.

Can I switch off the light ring at night? Yes, set the display brightness to off as described in section 5, or use Sleep mode, which dims it automatically.

Can I use the device in a basement or a garage? A dry basement is fine. Do not use the device in damp rooms, as described in the safety section, or in a garage while an engine is running, because it does not remove exhaust gases.

Does the device help against viruses and bacteria? The HEPA layer retains the aerosol droplets that carry viruses and bacteria, which lowers their concentration in the room air. The device is not a medical device and does not replace ventilation, distance or hygiene measures.

Can I leave the device running when nobody is at home? Yes. The device is designed for unattended operation and switches off by itself if it overheats or if the cover is opened.

How long will the device last? It is designed for at least eight years of daily use. The filter is the only part that wears out in normal use; the fan motor and the sensor are maintenance-free.

Why does the fan speed up at night when I am not in Sleep mode? Auto mode reacts to the air quality; cooking smells drifting in from another flat, a candle or a window opened for ventilation can raise the reading at any time. Use Sleep mode at night if you prefer a constant low speed.

Can I paint the housing or put stickers on it? No. Paint and adhesives can damage the housing and block the sensor opening or the outlet.

## 14. Technical glossary

CADR: the clean air delivery rate, the volume of filtered air the device delivers per hour. HEPA H13: a filter class that retains at least 99.95 percent of the most penetrating particle size. PM2.5: particulate matter smaller than 2.5 micrometres. VOC: volatile organic compounds, gases released by paint, cleaning products and furniture, which the carbon layer reduces. µg/m³: micrograms per cubic metre, the unit of the PM2.5 reading. dB: decibel, the unit of the noise level; a difference of 10 dB is perceived as roughly twice as loud.

## 15. Compliance

The Aria X200 complies with the applicable European directives for low-voltage equipment, electromagnetic compatibility, the restriction of hazardous substances and energy-related products, and carries the CE marking. The crossed-out wheeled bin symbol on the rating label means that the product must be collected separately at the end of its life (see the disposal section). The declaration of conformity is available from support on request.

### 15.1 Environmental information

The packaging consists of cardboard and a small amount of polyethylene foam, both of which can be recycled through household collection. The housing is made of recyclable polymer and the filter frame of cardboard; the filter media themselves are not recyclable and are treated as residual waste at the collection point. The device contains no refrigerants and no mercury. The manufacturer takes back used filters free of charge at the service centre for correct treatment.

## 16. Warranty

The Aria X200 comes with a 24-month limited warranty from the date of purchase. The warranty covers defects in materials and workmanship. It does not cover filters, damage caused by use outside this manual, water damage, or normal wear. To make a warranty claim, contact support with your proof of purchase and the serial number printed on the label under the device. ### 16.1 Warranty service procedure

Contact support before sending anything in; support will try to solve the problem remotely and otherwise issues a return number. Pack the device without the filter in the original packaging or in a sturdy box with padding, write the return number on the outside and include a copy of the proof of purchase. The service centre normally repairs or replaces the device within ten working days of receiving it and returns it at no cost to you. If the fault is not covered by the warranty, support sends a cost estimate before any work is done and the device is returned unrepaired if you decline.

## 17. Support

Email support@aria-air.example or call the support line Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 17:00. Spare parts, including filters and remote controls, can be ordered from the online shop.

### 17.1 Before you contact support

Have the following at hand: the serial number from the label under the device, the date and place of purchase, the error code or a description of the problem, and the steps you have already tried. Support answers most emails within two working days.

## 18. Disposal

Do not dispose of the device or used filters with household waste. Take them to a collection point for electronic equipment. Remove the batteries from the remote control before disposal.
