# Lumen Analytics — Employee Handbook

Version 3.2 · Effective 1 January 2026

## 1. Welcome

This handbook explains the main policies that apply to every employee of Lumen Analytics ("the Company"). It is not a contract. Where this handbook and your employment agreement differ, the employment agreement applies. Questions about any policy should go to People Operations (people@lumen-analytics.example).

### 1.1 How to use this handbook

The handbook is organised by topic: employment basics, time off, pay and expenses, conduct, learning, health and safety, the workplace, ways of working, data protection and leaving the Company. Each section states the rule, who approves exceptions and where to find the related form or tool. Local legal requirements in the countries where we employ people are summarised in country supplements on the intranet; where a supplement is stricter than this handbook, the supplement applies. Managers are expected to know this handbook and to apply it consistently.

### 1.2 Our values

Four values guide how we work. Curiosity: we ask why, test our assumptions with data and keep learning. Candour: we say what we think, respectfully and early, and we welcome being challenged. Care: we look after our customers, our colleagues and ourselves, and we take the long view. Craft: we take pride in work that is correct, clear and maintainable. The values are used in hiring, in performance reviews and in the way we give feedback to each other.

## 2. Employment basics

### 2.1 Probation period

New employees have a probation period of three months. During probation either side may end the employment with one week of notice. At the end of probation your manager holds a review meeting and confirms the position in writing.

### 2.2 Working hours

The standard working week is 40 hours, normally Monday to Friday between 09:00 and 18:00 with a one-hour lunch break. Core hours, when everyone is expected to be available, are 10:00 to 16:00. Outside core hours you may arrange your start and end times with your manager.

### 2.3 Remote work

Employees may work remotely up to three days per week. Tuesday is the company-wide office day and everyone is expected on site. Fully remote arrangements require written approval from the Head of People and are reviewed every six months. Remote employees receive a one-time home-office allowance of 400 EUR, paid with the first salary after the arrangement is approved.

### 2.4 Overtime and time recording

Overtime is work beyond the 40-hour week that your manager has asked for or approved in advance. It is compensated with time off in lieu at a rate of one hour off for every hour worked, to be taken within three months. If time off in lieu cannot be taken within that period, the hours are paid with the next salary at 125 percent of the normal hourly rate. Overtime should not exceed 10 hours per month on a regular basis; if a project needs more, your manager must agree a plan with People Operations first. All employees record their working time weekly in the HR portal, including remote days and days worked abroad. Managers approve the timesheets of their team by Monday noon for the previous week. Employees in roles that are exempt from time recording under local law are informed of this in their employment agreement.

### 2.5 Working from abroad

You may work from another country for up to 20 working days per calendar year, for example to extend a holiday or to visit family. Requests go to People Operations at least two weeks in advance and need the written agreement of your manager. Work abroad must take place on a company laptop connected through the company VPN, during hours that overlap with the core hours of your team for at least four hours per day. Longer stays, and work from a country where the Company has no legal entity for more than the days allowed, are not possible because of tax and social security rules. Business trips do not count towards the limit. Employees whose role requires access to restricted customer systems may be limited to certain countries; People Operations will tell you when you apply.

### 2.6 Internal mobility and secondments

Open positions are posted on the internal job board for at least one week before they are advertised externally. You may apply for an internal position after 12 months in your current role; tell your manager before the first interview so that the transition can be planned openly. Transfers take effect on the first day of a month, after a handover period agreed between the two managers, normally four to eight weeks. Temporary secondments to another team of up to six months are possible for development purposes and do not change your employment agreement; at the end of the secondment you return to your previous role unless all three parties agree otherwise.

### 2.7 Part-time work

Employees may request a reduction of their working hours to a minimum of 60 percent of full-time hours. Requests are considered in light of the needs of the team and answered in writing within four weeks. Salary, annual leave and the learning budget are prorated to the working hours. After parental leave, a temporary reduction to 80 percent for up to 12 months is approved automatically on request. Part-time employees take part in the company-wide office day when it falls on one of their working days.

### 2.8 Personal details

Keep your address, bank details and emergency contact up to date in the HR portal. Changes entered by the 15th of a month are reflected in that month's payroll; later changes take effect in the following month. Name changes require a copy of the official document. The emergency contact is used only if you cannot be reached during an emergency and is visible only to People Operations.

### 2.9 Work permits and visas

Candidates who need a work permit or visa are supported by People Operations and the Company's immigration adviser; the Company pays the application fees for the employee. The start date in the employment agreement is conditional on the permit being granted. Employees must inform People Operations at least three months before a permit or visa expires so that the renewal can be filed in time, and must not work on any day on which they do not hold a valid permit. Changes of role or working hours may require an updated permit; check with People Operations before agreeing to such a change.

### 2.10 Public office and political activity

Employees are free to engage in political and civic activity in their own time. Those who hold or stand for public office tell People Operations so that the time off required by law can be arranged; such time off is unpaid unless the law says otherwise. Company channels, the company name and company equipment may not be used for political campaigning, and colleagues may not be solicited for political donations at work.

## 3. Time off

### 3.1 Annual leave

Full-time employees receive 24 working days of paid annual leave per calendar year. Leave accrues at 2 days per month. New employees who start during the year receive leave in proportion to the months remaining. Up to 5 unused days may be carried over into the next year and must be used by 31 March; any days beyond that are lost.

### 3.2 Public holidays

Public holidays follow the calendar of the country where the employee is based. Employees who are asked to work on a public holiday receive one compensatory day off, to be taken within 60 days.

### 3.3 Sick leave

If you are unwell, inform your manager before 10:00 on the first day. A medical certificate is required from the fourth consecutive day of absence. The Company pays full salary for up to 30 sick days per year.

### 3.4 Parental leave

Birth and adoptive parents are entitled to 16 weeks of fully paid parental leave, which may be taken in up to two blocks within the first 12 months after the birth or adoption. Requests should be submitted at least 8 weeks in advance where possible.

### 3.5 Unpaid leave

After two years of service you may request unpaid leave of up to three months, for example for travel, study or family reasons. Requests need the approval of your manager and the Head of People and should be made at least two months in advance. During unpaid leave you keep your laptop and your access to company systems unless agreed otherwise, but annual leave does not accrue and the learning budget is suspended. Your position, or an equivalent one, is guaranteed when you return. Unpaid leave of more than one month is possible once every three years.

### 3.6 Sabbatical

After five years of continuous service you are entitled to a paid sabbatical of 4 weeks, which may be combined with annual leave. The sabbatical must be taken in one block within two years of becoming eligible and is available once every five years. Request it at least three months in advance so that your team can plan the cover. The sabbatical is meant for rest, travel or personal projects; it may not be used to work for another employer.

### 3.7 Volunteering days

Every employee has two paid volunteering days per year to support a registered charity or community project of their choice. Volunteering days are requested like annual leave and can be taken as single days or half days. Teams may also volunteer together, and team volunteering counts towards the two days. Volunteering days that are not used by the end of the year are lost and cannot be paid out.

### 3.8 Medical appointments and moving day

Medical appointments that cannot be scheduled outside working hours may be attended during the day without deduction, up to two hours per appointment; inform your manager in advance. Employees who move house receive one paid day off for the move, at most once per calendar year.

### 3.9 Religious holidays and days of significance

Employees may swap up to two public holidays per year for religious or cultural days that are important to them. Agree the swap with your manager at least two weeks in advance and record it in the HR portal so that payroll and the attendance system show the correct days.

### 3.10 Civic duties

Paid time off is granted for civic duties required by law, such as jury service or acting as an election official, for up to five working days per year. Provide the official summons to People Operations before the absence. Any compensation you receive from the authority for days paid by the Company is passed on to the Company.

### 3.11 Requesting leave

Request annual leave in the HR portal. Requests for more than three consecutive days should be submitted at least two weeks in advance; managers respond within five working days. Leave is approved on a first-come, first-served basis while keeping at least half of each team available. Leave that was already approved is not withdrawn except in an emergency, in which case the Company covers any cancellation costs that you cannot recover.

### 3.12 Caring responsibilities

Employees may take up to five paid days per year to care for a sick child or another dependent who lives with them, in addition to their own sick leave. Inform your manager on the first day and record the absence in the HR portal as care leave. Longer absences for caring reasons can be arranged as unpaid leave or as a temporary reduction of working hours under section 2.7.

## 4. Pay and expenses

### 4.1 Salary

Salaries are paid on the 25th of each month by bank transfer. Payslips are available in the HR portal. Salary reviews take place once a year, in February, with any change effective from 1 April.

### 4.2 Expenses

Business expenses are reimbursed when submitted through the expense tool within 30 days of the purchase, with a receipt attached. Single expenses above 250 EUR need prior written approval from your manager. Meals during business travel are reimbursed up to 45 EUR per day. Taxis are reimbursed only when public transport is not reasonably available or when travelling after 22:00.

### 4.3 Equipment

Every employee receives a laptop and a monitor. Equipment remains Company property and must be returned on the last working day. Lost or stolen equipment must be reported to IT within 24 hours.

### 4.4 Business travel

Book flights, trains and hotels through the travel tool so that the Company's insurance and negotiated rates apply. Choose rail for journeys of up to four hours. Flights are booked in economy class; premium economy is allowed for flights longer than six hours. Hotels are reimbursed up to 160 EUR per night, or up to 220 EUR per night in the high-cost cities listed in the travel tool. Travel time outside normal working hours is not counted as working time, except for employees who are required to drive. Every business trip is covered by the Company's travel insurance; the policy number and the emergency assistance line are printed on the back of your access badge. Combining a business trip with private travel is allowed if the private part does not increase the cost to the Company and your manager agrees in advance.

### 4.5 Private car use

Using your private car for business trips requires prior approval from your manager and a valid driving licence and insurance. Mileage is reimbursed at 0.30 EUR per kilometre, including fuel and wear, plus parking and toll costs with receipts. Fines and damage to the car are not reimbursed. Record the start and end points of each trip in the expense tool.

### 4.6 Benefits

Employees receive meal vouchers worth 8 EUR for each day worked, loaded onto a card at the start of the following month. Half of the cost of a monthly public transport pass is reimbursed, up to 60 EUR per month, through the expense tool. Employees who work mainly at a screen may claim an eye test every two years, up to 100 EUR. Details of all benefits and how to enrol are on the intranet under Benefits. Benefits are prorated for part-time employees in the same way as salary.

### 4.7 Relocation support

Employees who relocate at the Company's request, or who join from abroad, receive relocation support of up to 3,000 EUR against receipts for moving costs, temporary accommodation and travel. Relocation support must be approved by the Head of People before the move and is repaid in proportion if the employee leaves within 12 months of the move.

### 4.8 Company credit cards

Managers and employees who travel more than six times a year may apply for a company credit card. The card may be used only for business expenses, which still have to be submitted through the expense tool with receipts within 30 days. Personal use of the card is not permitted and leads to withdrawal of the card. Report a lost or stolen card to the issuing bank and to Finance immediately.

### 4.9 Payroll questions

Questions about your payslip go to payroll@lumen-analytics.example. Report any error within ten working days of receiving the payslip; confirmed underpayments are corrected by a separate transfer within five working days, and overpayments are deducted from the next salary unless another arrangement is agreed with Finance.

### 4.10 Job levels and pay transparency

Every role is assigned to one of six job levels, from L1 for entry positions to L6 for senior leadership. The level determines the salary band, the amount of autonomy expected and the criteria used in performance reviews. Salary bands for all levels are published on the intranet so that everyone can see how their pay relates to the band for their level. Promotions to a higher level are decided during the salary review in February on the basis of the performance reviews of the past year and a written case prepared by the manager. Employees may ask People Operations at any time how their salary is positioned within their band.

## 5. Conduct

### 5.1 Code of conduct

Treat colleagues, customers and partners with respect. Harassment, discrimination and retaliation are not tolerated and may lead to dismissal. Concerns can be raised with your manager, with People Operations, or anonymously through the reporting form linked on the intranet.

### 5.2 Confidentiality and data

Customer data may only be accessed for a documented business purpose. Never store customer data on personal devices or personal cloud accounts. Report any suspected data breach to security@lumen-analytics.example immediately; the security team must be able to notify affected customers within 72 hours.

### 5.3 Conflicts of interest

Outside employment or consulting that overlaps with the Company's business requires written approval from the Head of People. Gifts from suppliers or customers worth more than 50 EUR must be declined or reported.

### 5.4 Information security

Use the company password manager for all work accounts and enable multi-factor authentication wherever it is offered. Lock your screen whenever you leave your desk and never share your credentials, including with colleagues or IT. Install software only from the approved list in the IT portal; other tools need a request to IT, which is answered within three working days. Report phishing attempts with the Report button in the mail client rather than forwarding them. Connect to company systems only through the VPN when you are outside the office. Security awareness training is mandatory within the first month of employment and then once a year; access to customer systems is suspended if the training is overdue by more than 30 days.

### 5.5 Company systems and social media

Company laptops, email and chat are provided for work. Occasional personal use is acceptable as long as it does not interfere with your work or violate any policy. The Company may access work accounts for legitimate business reasons, such as during an investigation or when an employee is absent, following the procedure agreed with the employee representatives. On social media, make clear that your opinions are your own, never share confidential information or customer names, and do not comment on behalf of the Company unless Marketing has asked you to. Public talks or articles that mention customers or internal projects need approval from Marketing at least one week in advance.

### 5.6 Use of AI tools

Generative AI tools may be used for work only if they are on the approved list in the IT portal, which names the tools whose contracts prevent our data from being used for training. Customer data, personal data and source code must never be entered into tools outside that list. You remain responsible for anything you produce with AI assistance: check facts, numbers and licences before you use the output. Mark AI-generated text or images as such when they are shared externally.

### 5.7 Intellectual property

Work results created in the course of your employment, including code, documents, designs and inventions, belong to the Company as far as the law allows. Open-source contributions on company time or with company resources need approval from your manager and must use one of the licences on the approved list. Personal projects that do not use company time, equipment or confidential information remain your own; if a personal project is close to the Company's business, discuss it with your manager to avoid later disputes.

### 5.8 Alcohol, drugs and smoking

Do not drink alcohol during working hours or before driving on company business. Moderate consumption at company events is acceptable. The use or possession of illegal drugs on company premises or during work leads to dismissal. Smoking and vaping are allowed only in the designated outdoor areas.

### 5.9 Anti-bribery and hospitality

The Company does not pay or accept bribes, including facilitation payments to speed up routine procedures. Hospitality offered to customers or partners must be modest and work-related; meals or events above 100 EUR per person must be recorded in the hospitality register maintained by Finance before the invitation is sent. No gifts or hospitality may be offered to public officials. The rules for gifts you receive are in section 5.3.

### 5.10 Speaking up and whistleblowing

Anyone who suspects a breach of law or of this handbook, such as fraud, corruption, discrimination or a safety risk, can report it to their manager, to People Operations, or through the confidential whistleblowing channel operated by an external provider and linked on the intranet. Reports may be made anonymously. Every report is acknowledged within five working days and investigated within 20 working days where possible; the reporter is told when the investigation is closed. Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith is itself a serious breach of this handbook.

### 5.11 Disciplinary procedure

Minor issues are addressed first in a conversation between the employee and the manager. If the issue continues or is more serious, the Company follows a formal procedure: a written warning, then a final written warning, and finally dismissal. Gross misconduct, such as theft, violence or a serious breach of confidentiality, may lead to dismissal without prior warnings. Before any formal step the employee is invited to a meeting, receives the allegations in writing at least two working days beforehand and may be accompanied by a colleague. Written warnings expire after 12 months and are then removed from the personnel file.

### 5.12 Grievance procedure

If you have a concern about your work, your working conditions or your treatment by others that you could not resolve informally, you may raise a formal grievance in writing with People Operations. You will be invited to a meeting within 10 working days, may bring a colleague, and receive a written outcome within 10 working days after the meeting. You may appeal the outcome within five working days to the Head of People, whose decision is final. Raising a grievance in good faith never has negative consequences.

### 5.13 Working with customers

Many employees work directly with customers, on site or remotely. Before accessing a customer's systems or premises, read the customer's security and conduct requirements, which the account manager keeps in the project space, and sign any non-disclosure agreement the customer requires. On customer premises, follow the customer's rules on access, dress and working hours while they do not conflict with this handbook, and never discuss other customers. Feedback or complaints from customers are passed to the account manager the same day; disagreements with a customer are escalated internally rather than argued on site.

### 5.14 Media enquiries

All enquiries from journalists, analysts and bloggers are passed to Marketing without comment, including requests that seem harmless. Only the spokespeople named on the intranet speak to the media on behalf of the Company. If you are contacted directly, take the name and contact details of the person, say that someone from the Company will get back to them, and forward the request the same day.

## 6. Learning and development

Each employee has a learning budget of 1,200 EUR per year for courses, books and conferences, plus up to 5 paid learning days. Unused budget does not carry over. Conference attendance abroad must be approved by your manager at least 4 weeks in advance.

### 6.1 Performance reviews

Performance reviews take place twice a year, in April and October. Each review consists of a self-assessment, the manager's assessment and a conversation about goals for the next six months. Managers calibrate their assessments with their peers so that expectations are consistent across teams. The October review feeds into the salary review described in section 4.1. Ratings use four levels: developing, meets expectations, exceeds expectations and outstanding. Employees who receive a developing rating agree an improvement plan with their manager, with a check-in after three months. Review notes are stored in the HR portal and are visible to the employee, the manager and People Operations.

### 6.2 Mentoring programme

The mentoring programme matches employees with a more experienced colleague from another team for six months. Applications open in January and July. Mentoring pairs meet at least once a month during working hours. Mentors receive a short training session and may use up to two hours per month for the programme. Mentoring conversations are confidential unless both agree otherwise.

### 6.3 Certifications and study leave

Professional certifications relevant to your role are paid from the learning budget. Exam fees are reimbursed after the exam has been passed, and a second attempt is covered once. Employees receive two paid study days per certification in addition to the learning days described above. Certificates are recorded in the HR portal so that the Company can plan which skills it has in house.

### 6.4 Knowledge sharing

Every month the Company hosts Lumen Talks, an internal session where colleagues present projects, tools or lessons learned. Speakers may use up to four working hours to prepare. Recordings and slides are kept on the intranet for one year. New employees are encouraged to present what they learned in their first six months.

### 6.5 Onboarding

New employees receive a written onboarding plan before their first day and a buddy from another team who answers everyday questions during the first three months. The first week is reserved for setting up equipment, meeting the team and completing the mandatory trainings on security, data protection and the code of conduct. Managers hold check-ins after 30, 60 and 90 days; the 90-day check-in doubles as the probation review described in section 2.1. New employees are not expected to be fully productive before the end of their first quarter, and goals for the first months are written down together with the manager in the first two weeks.

## 7. Health, safety and wellbeing

### 7.1 Workstations

Everyone is responsible for a safe workplace. Office desks are height-adjustable and can be raised to standing height. Employees may request an ergonomic assessment of their desk, at home or in the office, from People Operations; recommended equipment up to 150 EUR, such as a separate keyboard, a mouse or a laptop stand, is provided by the Company. Take a short break away from the screen for at least five minutes every hour, and report pain or discomfort early rather than waiting for it to get worse.

### 7.2 Incidents and first aid

Report any accident, injury or near miss at work in the safety log on the intranet within 24 hours, however minor it seems. The names and phone numbers of the trained first aiders are posted next to the first-aid kits in every kitchen. Fire drills take place twice a year; follow the instructions of the evacuation wardens and gather at the assembly point shown on the evacuation plan by the lifts and stairs. Do not use the lifts during an evacuation.

### 7.3 Wellbeing support

The employee assistance programme offers confidential counselling by phone or video, 24 hours a day, free of charge, for work-related or personal issues, with up to six sessions per issue per year. The programme is run by an external provider, and the Company receives no information about who uses it. Managers are trained to recognise signs of stress and burnout and to refer colleagues to the programme. Workload concerns can also be raised in the regular one-to-one meetings with your manager.

### 7.4 Travel safety

Before travelling to a country for which a government travel warning exists, check with People Operations; some trips require a safety briefing and some are not permitted. Register every international trip in the travel tool so that you can be reached in an emergency. Keep the emergency assistance line of the travel insurance saved in your phone and carry your access badge, which has the number on the back.

### 7.5 Working safely from home

When working from home you are responsible for a safe workstation: a desk at a comfortable height, a chair with back support, a screen at eye level and enough light. The Company provides the laptop and monitor described in section 4.3 and, on request, the same ergonomic assessment that is offered in the office. Report accidents that happen while working from home in the safety log in the same way as accidents in the office. Keep company equipment and printed documents out of reach of other people in your household and lock the laptop when you leave it.

## 8. Workplace and facilities

### 8.1 Desk booking

The office uses shared desks. Book a desk in the desk booking tool on the intranet, at the latest by 18:00 the day before. Bookings that are not checked in by 10:30 are released for others. Teams have a home zone where their members are booked first, but anyone may book any free desk. Personal belongings go into your locker at the end of the day so that the desk is clear for the next person.

### 8.2 Visitors

Visitors must be registered in the visitor system at least 24 hours in advance by the employee who invited them, and must wear a visitor badge while in the office. Visitors are accompanied at all times and are not allowed in areas marked restricted. Customer visits should be announced to the office manager so that a meeting room and refreshments can be prepared.

### 8.3 Meeting rooms

Meeting rooms are booked through the calendar. Release a room as soon as you know you will not need it; rooms that are not used within 15 minutes of the start of a booking may be taken by others. Quiet rooms are for individual focused work and calls and cannot be booked for more than two hours at a time. Leave rooms as you found them: wipe the whiteboard, take your cups with you and switch off the screen.

### 8.4 Kitchen and shared spaces

Coffee, tea, water and fruit are provided free of charge. Label food in the fridge with your name and the date; the fridges are emptied every Friday at 17:00. Load the dishwasher instead of leaving cups in the sink, and start it when it is full. Keep phone calls out of the kitchen during lunchtime.

### 8.5 Access badges and office hours

Your access badge is personal; do not lend it to anyone and do not let people you do not know follow you through the door. Report a lost badge to the office manager immediately so that it can be blocked; a replacement is issued the same day. Offices are open to employees from 07:00 to 21:00 on working days; outside these hours and at weekends, access requires prior notice to the office manager.

### 8.6 Contractors and temporary staff

External contractors and agency staff receive a time-limited badge and account, sponsored by the manager who engaged them. They follow the conduct, security and data protection rules of this handbook while working for the Company, but the sections on pay, leave and benefits do not apply to them. Managers review contractor access every three months and end it on the last day of the engagement.

### 8.7 Deliveries and post

Business post and parcels are received at reception and announced to the addressee by chat. Private parcels may not be sent to the office. Outgoing business post is placed in the tray at reception by 15:00 for same-day dispatch; couriers for urgent documents are booked through the office manager and charged to the team's cost centre.

## 9. Communication and ways of working

### 9.1 Tools and response times

Chat is used for quick questions and team coordination, email for external communication and anything that needs a record, and the project tool for tasks. Respond to chat messages within the same working day and to emails within two working days; set a status or an out-of-office message when you are away. Nobody is expected to read messages outside their working hours, and managers should not send messages that imply otherwise.

### 9.2 Meetings

Every meeting has an owner, an agenda shared in advance and written notes afterwards. Wednesday afternoons are meeting-free across the Company so that everyone has time for focused work. Recurring meetings are reviewed every quarter and cancelled if they are no longer useful. Recording a meeting requires the consent of everyone attending. Default meeting lengths are 25 and 50 minutes so that people have time to move between meetings.

### 9.3 Decisions and documentation

Decisions that affect more than one team are recorded in a short decision document on the intranet, stating the context, the options considered, the decision and who made it. Project documentation lives in the project space, not in personal drives, so that it remains available when people change roles. The company language for written documentation is English; local languages are used where the law or the audience requires it.

### 9.4 Company events

The whole Company meets twice a year: at the summer offsite in June and at the year-end gathering in December. Attendance is during working time and travel costs are covered. Each team also has a budget of 60 EUR per person per quarter for team events, which the manager approves. Partners and families are invited to the year-end gathering.

### 9.5 Feedback and one-to-ones

Every employee has a regular one-to-one meeting with their manager, weekly or every two weeks, which belongs to the employee: it is the place to raise workload, development and concerns. Feedback is given early, in private and with concrete examples, and it goes in both directions; managers ask their teams for feedback on their own work at least twice a year. Praise may be given in public, criticism never is.

### 9.6 Internal communications

The whole Company meets online on the first Thursday of every month for the all-hands meeting, where leadership shares results and plans and answers questions submitted in advance or live. A weekly digest email summarises the news of the week, and the intranet front page carries announcements that affect everyone. Important changes, including changes to this handbook, are always announced in these channels rather than only in chat.

### 9.7 Distributed teams

Teams whose members work in different locations meet in person at least once per quarter, normally for two days, in one of the Company's offices; travel and accommodation are booked through the travel tool and follow the travel rules in section 4.4. Meetings that include remote participants are run so that everyone can take part equally: one person in the room shares the screen, questions are taken from the chat first, and decisions are written down during the meeting. Teams agree on a shared set of working hours in which everyone is reachable, taking time zones into account.

## 10. Data protection

### 10.1 Employee data

The Company processes employee data for payroll, benefits, performance management and legal obligations. You can see most of your data in the HR portal and request a full copy or a correction from the data protection officer at dpo@lumen-analytics.example; requests are answered within 30 days. Employee data is shared with external providers, such as the payroll provider, only under a contract that restricts its use to the agreed purpose.

### 10.2 Customer data

Customer and partner data are governed by section 5.2 and by the data protection guideline on the intranet. Customer data may be processed only in the systems listed in the data inventory. Exports to spreadsheets or local files are not permitted without the approval of the data protection officer. Access rights are reviewed every quarter by the system owners, and access is removed on the day an employee changes role or leaves.

### 10.3 Records retention

Finance and payroll records are kept for seven years after the end of the financial year. Recruitment records of unsuccessful candidates are deleted six months after the position is filled. Personnel files are kept for three years after the end of employment and then deleted, unless a legal claim is pending. Chat messages are retained for one year and email for five years, after which they are deleted automatically.

### 10.4 Monitoring

The Company does not monitor the content of employees' work or private communication in the normal course of business. Access logs of company systems are kept for security purposes and are reviewed only when there is a specific reason, such as a suspected security incident or a complaint, and only by the security team or People Operations following the procedure agreed with the employee representatives. Cameras at the office entrances record for security; the footage is kept for 30 days and then deleted unless it is needed for an investigation.

## 11. Leaving the Company

The notice period after probation is one month for employees and two months for managers. Accrued but unused annual leave is paid out with the final salary. An exit interview with People Operations is offered to everyone who leaves.

### 11.1 Handover and garden leave

Employees who resign agree a handover plan with their manager in the first week of the notice period, covering open tasks, documentation and the transfer of access rights. The Company may place an employee on garden leave for part or all of the notice period; during garden leave, salary and benefits continue, but the employee does not work and does not access company systems.

### 11.2 References and rehiring

A reference letter stating your role, period of employment and main responsibilities is issued within two weeks of your last working day on request. Former employees are welcome to apply again; rehiring is possible six months after the departure date, and previous service counts towards the sabbatical entitlement.

## 12. Changes to this handbook

The Company may update this handbook at any time. Material changes are announced by email at least two weeks before they take effect. The current version is always available on the intranet.
